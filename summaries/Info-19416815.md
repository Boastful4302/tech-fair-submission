**Behavioral Response to Dead Ants**

• Argentine ants (Linepithema humile) exhibit a stereotyped behavior known as necrophoresis, removing dead colony members from their nest.

**Decomposition and Chemical Signals**

• Decomposition products like fatty acids can trigger both necrophoric behavior and foraging responses depending on the nest's current activities.
• Ant workers often carry freshly killed (dead <1 h) ants to refuse piles before significant decomposition occurs.

**Cuticular Chemistry Changes After Death**

• The cuticular chemistry of living and newly deceased Argentine ant workers undergoes rapid changes after death, specifically within 1 hour.
• Two compounds, dolichodial and iridomyrmecin, characteristic of living ants, disappear from the cuticle shortly after death.

**Recognition and Necrophoresis**

• Dead ants are recognized by their absence of certain chemical signals (dolichodial and iridomyrmecin) which triggers necrophoresis.
