**Camponotus anderseni Mangrove Ant Resilience to Hypoxic Conditions**

• The Camponotus anderseni mangrove ant employs a soldier's head to block the nest entrance, preventing water intrusion and maintaining an internal environment conducive to its unique physiological adaptations.

**Gas Exchange Limitations**

• In the absence of gas exchange in the nest cavity, CO2 concentrations can reach >30% and O2 levels fall below 1%, leading to hypoxic conditions.
• Oxygen uptake is significantly affected by these conditions:
	+ Under normal circumstances (no CO2 absorption), O2 uptake decreases linearly until 18%.
	+ With CO2 absorption, O2 uptake follows a three-part pattern:
		- Initial linear decrease
		- Exponential decrease to 4%
		- Final exponential decrease with smaller slope

**Anaerobic Respiration**

• The respiratory quotient (RQ) increases from 0.92 to above 1 when CO2 concentration exceeds 15-17%, indicating a shift towards anaerobic respiration.
• Notably, the Camponotus anderseni mangrove ant initiates anaerobic respiration at high CO2 levels, marking a first for social insects and highlighting its remarkable adaptability.
