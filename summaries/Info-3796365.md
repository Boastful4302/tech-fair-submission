**Natural History of Allergic Reactions to Jack Jumper Ant Stings**

• A study examined the natural history of allergic reactions to "jack jumper ant" (Myrmecia pilosula) stings in a population of 400,000.
• Key findings:
	+ 49 people experienced an immediate generalized reaction from their first sting.
	+ Of these 49, 31 had a generalized reaction to further stings and 18 others developed a generalized reaction on subsequent stings.
• Trend observed: local reactions at the sting site often became generalized to the entire body.
• Counterintuitive finding: of 15 people who showed no reaction to their first sting, 12 later developed a generalized reaction on subsequent stings.

**Methodological Consideration**

• Self-selection bias may have led to an overestimation of the frequency of generalized reactions.
