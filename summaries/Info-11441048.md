**Navigational Behavior of Wood Ants**

* Wood ants (Formica japonica) foraged between their nest and feeding site, traveling a 7.9m route across a wide terrace platform with a prominent landmark panorama.
* Displacement from the training route up to 11m sideways resulted in ants returning paths intersecting at approximately 13m behind the nest, indicating reliance on distant landmarks.
* Research findings suggest that concealing part of the landmark panorama had two distinct effects:
	+ Shielding only the lower section had no effect
	+ Shielding the entire skyline above a certain elevation altered the ants' behavior
* Disrupting the familiar skyline panorama caused ants to move in the home direction for 0.1-0.4m before initiating a search programme.
* The study suggests that wood ants use distant sky features as navigational guidemarks, rather than relying on chemical or celestial cues.
