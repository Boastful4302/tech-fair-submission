**Dorylomorph Phylogeny and Evolution**

* The dominant invertebrate predators in tropical and subtropical ecosystems are army ants (belonging to the Dorylomorph group of ants).
* A phylogenetic analysis of 11 nuclear genes, combined with fossil evidence, estimated the divergence times of major Dorylomorph lineages.
* Key findings include:
	+ Non-monophyly of one subfamily and several genera within dorylomorphs
	+ Proposal to merge non-monophyletic groups into a single monophyletic subfamily: Dorylinae
* Estimated crown age of the group Dorylinae: 87 (74-101) million years.
* Phylogenetic analyses:
	+ Questioned army ant monophyly, providing weak support for their evolutionary history
	+ Revealed non-monophyly of the genus Cerapachys, with multiple lineages scattered across the Dorylinae tree
	+ Recovered relationships among Cerapachys-like clades and other doryline genera with strong support
* Notable cases:
	+ The genus Sphinctomyrmex consists of two separate lineages with convergent morphologies: one in the Old World tropics and the other in the New World tropics.
