**Fire Ant Rafts**

• Fire ants (Solenopsis invicta) can form large rafts by linking their bodies together to create a waterproof structure.
• The ants' ability to form a raft is analogous to weaving a hydrophobic fabric, allowing them to float for extended periods of time.
• A single ant struggles in water, but a group of ants can remain buoyant and even travel long distances while afloat.
• The rafts are formed through the ants' self-assembled body-linking behavior, creating a hydrophobic surface that repels water.

**Raft Construction**

• The construction rate of the raft has been modeled based on observations of ant trajectories atop the raft.
• Ants at the edge of the raft appear to be trapped by their neighbors, suggesting that "cooperative" behaviors may rely on coercion rather than voluntary actions.
