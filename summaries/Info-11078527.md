**Fossil Record and Evolutionary History**

• A 92 Ma worker ant found in New Jersey amber has an acidopore, classifying it as a member of the Formicinae subfamily.
• This fossil is the only known Cretaceous record of the Formicinae subfamily and one of two unequivocally identified fossils from the Cretaceous period that belong to extant ant subfamilies (alongside Brownimecia and Canapone).
• The preserved morphology suggests a basal position in the Formicinae subfamily.

**Divergence and Abundance**

• Mid-Cretaceous existence of Formicinae (105-110 Ma) and Ponerinae indicates divergence of ants.
• Sphecomyrminae may be either the sister group or a paraphyletic stem group to all other ants.
• Ant abundance suggests they did not become common in terrestrial ecosystems until the Eocene (45 Ma).

**Colony Size and Emergence**

• Coinciding with the emergence of modern genera, large colonies of at least 10,000 individuals formed during the Eocene.
