**Acoustic Communication in Ant Colonies**

• Ants continuously produce barely audible stridulatory sounds within their colonies.
• Initial research suggested transmission through substrate, but this theory is disputed due to:
	+ Ineffective signal propagation through solid media
	+ Limited understanding of substrate dynamics
• Alternative explanation: ants likely communicate primarily through air using hairlike sensilla on antennae that respond to particle sound velocity.
• Key characteristics of acoustic communication in ant colonies include:
	+ Near-field scale of approximately 100 mm, where particle sound velocity is enhanced and steep gradients aid source direction and distance determination.
	+ Tracheal air sacs in Solenopsis richteri may amplify radiation of stridulatory sounds.
