**Eusocial Hymenoptera and Interspecific Mating**

• Eusocial Hymenoptera can benefit from interspecific mating due to their haplodiploid genetic system and reproductive division of labor in females.
• Interspecifically mated queens produce viable sons that mate intraspecifically, offsetting fitness losses through female offspring.

**Benefits of Hybrid Offspring**

• Hybrid workers provide advantages, such as superior ability to occupy marginal habitats with low intraspecific competition.
• Queens trade expected reproductive success for a higher probability of achieving some reproductive success by mating interspecifically.

**Sperm Parasitism and Hybridization**

• Females that mate interspecifically can be considered "sperm parasites" on males of the other species.
• Sperm parasitism is linked to widespread hybridization among Acanthomyops (Lasius) ants in North America and may be a key driver of hybridization phenomena in ants.
