**Thermoregulation in Ants and Termites**

• Temperature is an important factor for ant colony fitness, with benefits (e.g. increased reproduction rate) and costs (e.g. higher energy expenditure)
• Social insects can regulate not only their own body temperature but also the temperature of their surroundings through nest construction and behavioral interactions
• Two main thermoregulatory strategies in mound building ants: moving brood according to thermal gradients, or keeping stable temperatures

**Thermoregulation Strategies**

• Dry nests use solar radiation, ant metabolism, and nest insulation to maintain temperature (0.15–4.3 W per nest)
• Wet nests rely on microbial activity for heat production (24–30 W per nest), in addition to ant metabolism and solar heating
• Microbial activity can be estimated by calculating the respiration rate of nest material, which is higher in summer (up to 35 times)

**Ant Thermoregulation Examples**

• Formica rufa group: maintains a heat core with high and stable temperatures in their nests
• Eciton hamatum and E. burchelli regulate temperature in their temporary swarms (bivouacs) to optimize brood development
• Solenopsis invicta: moves brood daily to places with optimal temperatures

**Thermoregulation in Termites**

• Coptotermes acinaciformis uses externally or internally driven ventilation to regulate nest temperature
• Macrotermes bellicosus and Amitermes merionalis build nests with a stable temperature core maintained by metabolism and soil mound structure

**General Trends**

• Low insulative properties as solar collectors (increasing thermal gradient for brood development)
• High inner temperatures maintained through low thermal conductivity, good insulation, and metabolic heat production
