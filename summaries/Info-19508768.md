**Urbanization and Cavity-Nesting Ants**

• Urbanization can have a significant impact on biodiversity, particularly concerning insects.
• Research on urban environments has shown that cavity-nesting sites for ants are often limited.
• A study in Toledo, OH compared the availability of cavity-nesting sites in three types of urban habitats:
	+ Gardens
	+ Vacant lots
	+ Forests
• Artificial nests were created to investigate the impact of nest-site limitation on ant communities:
	+ 72 large hollow twigs (83 by 6 mm)
	+ 72 small hollow twigs (140 by 2 mm)
	+ 72 spherical hollow cavities (6.52-31.1 cm^3 in volume, 1-mm opening)
• Key findings:
	+ Cavity-nesting ant occupancy rates varied among different types of artificial and natural nests.
	+ Small twigs accounted for the majority of natural nests (81.1%).
	+ Ants occupied a small percentage of available nest sites, ranging from 4.1-14.6%.
• Comparison between artificial and natural nests revealed differences in cavity-nesting ant occupancy rates:
	+ Spherical nests were less commonly used by ants than other types of nests.
	+ Large twigs were also less frequently used compared to small twigs.

**Limitations and Opportunities**

• Further research is needed to understand the impact of urbanization on cavity-nesting ant populations.
• The availability and limitation of cavity-nesting sites in urban environments remain understudied.
