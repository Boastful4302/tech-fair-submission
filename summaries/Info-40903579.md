**Xenoparasitic Reproduction in Messor Ibericus**

• Ants of the species Messor ibericus have a unique reproductive system that allows them to produce same-species offspring, but also enables them to lay individuals from two distinct species.
• This is achieved through the cloning of males from another species by female ants, resulting in cloned males with distinct genomes and morphologies.

**Evolutionary History**

• The evolutionary history of this reproductive mode suggests a shift from sexual parasitism to cross-species cloning over 5 million years ago.
• Females exhibiting this reproductive mode are termed xenoparous, indicating that they give birth to other species as part of their life cycle.

**Key Features**

• Cloning of males by female ants is a key feature of this system, enabling the production of individuals with distinct genetic and morphological characteristics.
• This reproductive strategy has evolved over millions of years, resulting in a unique life cycle characterized by cross-species cloning.
