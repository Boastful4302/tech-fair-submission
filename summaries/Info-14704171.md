**Gp-9 Gene and Social Organization in Fire Ants**

• The Gp-9 gene, an odorant-binding protein gene, is associated with social organization in fire ants, particularly monogyny (single queen) or polygyny (multiple queens).
• Amino acid substitutions in this gene have been linked to the expression of monogyny or polygyny in Solenopsis richteri clade.
**Gp-9 Gene in S. geminata**

• The Gp-9 gene was studied in S. geminata, a distantly related fire ant species.
• In S. geminata, the Gp-9 gene is identical in both polygyne and monogyne forms.
**Genetic Differences between Polygyne and Monogyne Forms of S. geminata**

• Polygyne S. geminata has lower genetic diversity compared to the monogyne form.
• The polygyne form of S. geminata has different gene frequencies compared to the monogyne form.
**Origin of Polygyne Form in S. geminata**

• The polygyne form of S. geminata is thought to have originated from a local monogyne population through a founder event.
**Loss of Allelic Variation and Breakdown in Discrimination Abilities**

• Loss of allelic variation at genes encoding recognition cues may be responsible for the breakdown in discrimination abilities leading to acceptance of multiple queens in colonies.
